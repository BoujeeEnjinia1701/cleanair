# 0003: STEM fixes

Status: made under Amish's standing instruction of 2026-10-23 (STANDARDS section 20, fix rule); Amish can reverse them.

## F1: scope is shrouded fans and duct openings (CAR-STM-001 X3)

The team deck lists ceiling fans. Ceiling fans have 29 to 54 in spans (ENERGY STAR) and no shroud, and develop almost no static pressure; a 12 to 24 in collar cannot reach the rotor and has nothing to seal to, so air would pass round any panel. Options weighed: (a) drop ceiling fans from the scope; (b) a ceiling-mounted shroud and panel, which is a different product with its own structure and fixings over occupants. Chosen: (a). Cost: none. The principle (an existing fan drives air through bought media) is kept.

## F2: UV-C strip removed (CAR-STM-001 X4)

Dose = mean fluence rate x residence time. A low-cost strip at the fan face (6 x 10 mW, 0.10 m path) gives about 0.003 mJ/cm2, 1 % single pass against an estimated SARS-CoV-2 D90 of 0.5 mJ/cm2 in air (Allen, Benner and Bahnfleth 2021), adding 0.07 eACH to a ward. An 18 W high-power strip with a generous 0.30 m path gives about 0.16 mJ/cm2, and each 40 to 65 mW LED reaches the ACGIH limit (3 mJ/cm2 effective) in 7 to 11 minutes at 1 m. Options weighed: (a) remove the module; (b) a high-power enclosed module (cost, an interlock, IEC 62471 and ozone tests); (c) keep the strip. Chosen: (a); (b) is proposed to Amish as a separate product (CAR-DEC-001 D5). Saving: the strip, its driver and wiring; the kit now has no electrical part.

## F3: hold-down straps on the fan frame (CAR-STM-001 S23)

Found by the independent review: straps across the outlet guard could press a thin guard toward the blades. The straps now cross the solid fan frame 261 mm from the centre, outside the 484 mm guard opening. Cost: none.
